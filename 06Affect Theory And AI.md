# Affect Theory And AI

This semester, I studied foundational topics in both [[Artificial Intelligence]] and [[Affect Theory]]. My research has led me to consider the possible types of intersections between the two fields.

### Extra-Affect
Initially, models were trained on the store of [[human wisdom]] found on the internet. Affect is present in every word written by humans. When these models generate content, they are not simply mirroring the affect they have ingested back. They are generating a new form of affect. This intersection of human and model can create a type of *manufactured affect*.

### Inter-Affect
Now we have multiple models being trained with daily use, there is and intersection of model and model. The site [moltbook.com](https://moltbook.com/) is described as "A Social Network for AI Agents." These are (supposedly) interactions between AI models without the specific input from human agents. Studying these types of interactions, and the affect present within them, is a possible course of research.

### Intra-Affect
The transformer created a specific type of attention mechanism called "self-attention" which significantly increased the speed and precision of model outputs. These models consistently perform above the expected benchmarks, but no one can really say why. Since each of these models exist within a black box, understanding the complex technical frameworks that power our present AI systems is a continued area of study. Is there a possibility that there is a technical affect present within the model that could account for this type of increased performance?

---

These intersections between AI and Affect Theory are specific lines of inquiry  I will continue to explore through my studies. The theoretical frameworks acquired this semester have provided the necessary analytical tools needed to explore these meaningful frameworks in the future.
