# reasoning

## Definition
Reasoning in [artificial intelligence (AI)](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/artificial-intelligence) refers to the mechanism of using available information to generate [predictions](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/predictive-ai), make [inferences](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/ai-inference) and draw conclusions. It involves representing data in a form that a machine can process and understand, then applying logic to arrive at a decision.

Reasoning in AI is depicted as a system typically made up of two core components:

    ● **Knowledge base**

    ● **Inference engine**

### Knowledge base

The knowledge base is the backbone of an AI reasoning system. It contains knowledge graphs, ontologies, semantic networks and other models of knowledge representation. These structured forms map real-world entities—such as concepts, domain-specific information, events, facts, objects, relationships, rules and situations—into a structure that AI models can process and understand.

### Inference engine

Acting as the brain of an AI reasoning system is the [inference](https://research.ibm.com/blog/AI-inference-explained) engine. It’s powered by trained [machine learning](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/machine-learning) models. The inference engine implements the needed logic and reasoning methods to analyze data from the knowledge base and reach a decision.
