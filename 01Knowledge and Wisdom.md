# Knowledge and Wisdom

Humans have an amazing capacity for learning and language. Although other species communicate, humans develop new skills and adapt to new information. This special quality may be the reason humans are innate learners. Each person creates an individual map of experiences built on the foundational human genetics. The brain consists of a massive number of connections, not unlike a computer network. The paths between the connections are constantly changing - becoming stronger when reinforced by repetition or reward and fading with disuse.

 While we are not the only species to communicate, we do so with a distinct advantage. We can adapt. We can take past experiences and use those to generate new knowledge. We are adaptable, we are flexible. We can encounter novel situations and predict behaviors. While most of us take these types of cognitive tasks for granted, the earliest thinkers wanted to know how we created and stored knowledge.

 In ancient Greece, the earliest philosophers studied how the senses and the mind interacted to generated knowledge. Two separate schools of thought gained prominence:

- The [[empiricists]] believed knowledge was acquired through experience and learning came through interacting with the senses. 
- The [[rationalists]] believed knowledge could be codified and followed predetermined rules which allowed the world to be understood through the power of reasoning (logos). 

As modern computing as born, we continued to apply these ideas to create the *thinking machines* theorized by Alan Turing. Boy have we come a long way.
