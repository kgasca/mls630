# The Affective Turn

What is affect theory? That’s not an easy question to answer.  

Brian Massumi (2002) writes about a separate, autonomous state that exists between easily defined dualities of before and after. This is the energy you are aware of at the periphery of your being, but you may not have a name for. It happens at a pre-linguistic, pre-cognitive level. And unlike the formal language of computer systems, it is not binary.

Sara Ahmed (2010), however, describes affect as not just a feeling but as something “sticky” that attaches itself to objects and ideas. She describes affective states, like happiness, as being tied to societal expectations. The state of happiness is tied to objects or ideas deemed worthy of pursuing. And those that do not follow these expectations are known as “affect aliens.” 

Affect itself is intangible and its definition imprecise. It is a contagious energy that flows between objects prior to conscious thought or feeling. It also exists in the liminal flux of that dual state of being - that of affecting and being affected.  It exists in the moment of happening and is transmitted into the ether that surrounds us. But is it transmitted to the generative AI models used today?
