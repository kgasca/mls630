# The Affective Turn

What is [[affect theory]]? That’s not an easy question to answer.  There are probably as many definitions of affect as there are affect theorists.  

Some, like Brennan, state the affect exists in olfactory and other nervous system components. There is a physical and unconscious call and response in the body and between bodies.  

Sara Ahmed, however, describe affect as not just a feeling but as something  “sticky” that attaches itself to objects and ideas. 

>Affect is what sticks, or what sustains or preserves the connection between ideas, values and objects (30)

In her article, she describes affective states as being tied to societal expectations. The state of happiness is tied to objects or ideas deemed worthy of pursuing. 


Brian Massumi writes about a separate, autonomous state that exists between easily defined dualities of before and after which he describes 

> ... a third state, an excluded middle, prior to the distinction between activity and passivity: affect (35)

This is the energy you are aware of at the periphery of your being, but you may not have a name for. It happens at a pre-linguistic level. And unlike the [[formal language]] of computer systems, it is not binary. It operates outside of the known active/passive coin sides and in fact happens prior to the categorization of an event.


Affect itself is intangible and its definition imprecise. It is a contagious energy that flows between objects prior to conscious thought or feeling.  It also exists in the liminal flux of that dual states of being: affecting and being affected.  It exists in the moment of happening and is transmitted into the ether that surrounds us.
