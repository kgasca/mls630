# workplace affects

## Definition
Gregg describes a workplace no longer widely experienced. While some components are relatable to today's working force, our post-COVID world has settled into a more hybrid world where physical presence is not a given. The rise of snark — Gregg discusses how workplace anonymity gives rise to a type of alienation that allows for the creation of "snark." This passive aggressive type of communication is seen as an "unfortunate consequence" of the inherent disconnectedness in the office cubicle universe. Since we do not actually work to create solid connections with office mates and coworkers, there is a "simulation of affect" that binds the world together.

This idea of snark is tied to the main method of workplace communication — emails — which by their nature of rigid adherence to grammatical forms and business language make for a built-in equality among the parties. Snark can truly only exist within the twin constraints of anonymity and equality. This can also only be achieved when "language is the instrument of production" and "information labor" is a key component of office politics.

The differences between the office space described in this article and today's workplace is the given presence of bodies. The simulation of affect occurs in light of the physical adjacency of those in an office. When the physical adjacency is removed, where does the affect go? What she describes as "pure gesture" exists because of the expectation of physical nearness. As she states, "it is the action of bodies that force colleagues to feel."

So where would affect exist within a computer model? If we take the concept of self-attention and relate it to self-affect, what would that mean?
